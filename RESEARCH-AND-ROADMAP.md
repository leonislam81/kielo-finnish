# Kielo: research and product plan

## Product idea

**Kielo** is a calm, visual Finnish-learning web app for English- and Bengali-speaking beginners. The interface is in English. Learners can switch lesson explanations between English and Bangla while Finnish examples, audio, and practice stay central.

The name comes from *kielo*, the Finnish name for lily-of-the-valley, Finland's national flower. The visual direction uses lake blue, pine green, snow, soft berry red, and editorial photography inspired by Finnish seasons.

## Research findings

- The Finnish National Agency for Education (OPH) maps YKI grades 1–6 to CEFR A1–C2. YKI is arranged in basic, intermediate, and advanced bands, with a separate assessment for listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
- OPH describes YKI as practical language use and gives recurring themes such as personal background, home, services, culture, travel, health, work, environment, and society. Those themes provide the real-life spine for the course.
- The Council of Europe's CEFR Companion Volume includes Pre-A1 and updated descriptors. Kielo therefore starts before A1 and treats progress as communication tasks rather than a list of grammar labels.
- Yle provides beginner Finnish videos (alphabet, numbers, phrases), subtitled programmes, simplified news, and levelled self-study materials. Kielo links learners to these resources rather than copying or rehosting third-party media.
- Aalto OpenLearning's beginner Finnish course introduces long and short vowels and double consonants. The University of Turku's Finnish teaching videos include pronunciation, vowel harmony, and short/long sound practice. Kielo's Sound Lab turns the first pronunciation ideas into short browser-audio drills and keeps the university resources linked as optional follow-up.
- VR's official ticket flow labels one-way and return options as Meno and Paluu, and Helsinki station departure listings use raide / raiteelta for platform information. These terms inform the station role-play; the lesson does not present live train times.
- The Finnish Meteorological Institute explains the regional variation of Finnish seasons, including the longer northern winter and the midnight sun. Finland's official country communications describe Midsummer, sauna, cottages, and outdoor life. These become the settings for culture-based examples.

## Course map: Pre-A1 to C2

| Level | Learner milestone | Sample course units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-A1 | Recognise sounds and use first survival phrases | Sounds & spelling; greetings; numbers and time; first questions |
| A1 | Handle simple everyday exchanges | Introductions; café and shop; home and family; directions and transport |
| A2 | Describe routines and past events; understand everyday texts; write concise messages and notes; handle short everyday conversations | Daily life; weather and seasons; health; travel; messages, notices, invitations, polite requests, and suggestions |
| B1 | Manage familiar situations with connected speech | Work and study; housing; appointments; stories and opinions |
| B2 | Explain ideas and participate in extended discussion | Society; environment; media; formal requests and workplace communication |
| C1–C2 | Understand nuanced Finnish and express precise, flexible ideas | Register and style; argument; idioms; long-form listening and writing |

Each unit is designed around a scene, a short input (image, dialogue, or official video link), a compact vocabulary set, one grammar pattern, four-skills practice, and a short review. The A2 Reading practice uses short messages, a library notice, and a travel note; learners can tap words for English or Bangla meanings and answer detail questions. It links to Yle Selkouutiset for optional real-world reading. A2 Writing guides learners through arranging Finnish message parts for a reply, a cottage invitation, and a short request. A2 Speaking rehearses arranging a meeting, suggesting a new time, asking politely for help, and finding a train platform. A2 Grammar Lab adds short, contextual practice for the imperfect, place direction, polite conditional requests, and the connector koska. These grammar examples complement OPH A2.1 descriptors for familiar everyday interaction, requests, invitations, and suggestions; they are a focused practice set rather than a complete grammar syllabus. The B1 Listening Lab builds on that foundation with connected stories about work, changed plans, and travel; questions ask learners to follow the main point and notice a detail. Its goals follow OPH's B1.1 listening description, and built-in audio is identified as browser speech; learners can continue with recorded Finnish from Yle Selkouutiset. The B1 Reading Lab adds a neighbourhood notice, a work email, and a winter day-trip story, with tap-for-help vocabulary, English or Bangla translations, comprehension questions, and optional browser audio. It follows OPH's B1.1 reading goal of understanding the gist and some details in clear written Finnish. Speaking practice uses scene imagery, English and Bangla instructions, audio, optional browser voice recognition, and self-practice; recognition is not presented as a pronunciation score. The B1 Speaking Lab adds four Finland-set role-plays for giving an opinion with a reason, explaining a delay, checking a deadline, and describing a missing word. Their goals follow OPH’s B1.1 everyday interaction and communication-strategy descriptors. The B1 Grammar Lab adds contextual practice for relative clauses, the perfect tense, contrast with vaikka, and purpose with jotta. These are focused practice examples for building connected ideas, not a complete B1 grammar syllabus. The app tracks a local progress record so learners can start without an account. Other iterations add visual word matching, a small spaced-review schedule that learners can adjust with Again or Got it, guided café and train-station conversations with listen-and-choose turns, and a standalone Sound Lab for the eight vowels and sound length.

## Current course additions

The B1 Writing Lab adds three guided connected texts: explaining a work delay, sharing an opinion with a reason and making a plan, and recounting a winter day trip. Sentence-block ordering scaffolds clear writing about familiar everyday topics, in line with OPH’s B1.1 written-production description; it is guided practice, not open-ended writing assessment or an official YKI task.

The B2 Writing Lab completes the four-skill practice path through B2 with three scaffolded texts: a balanced city newsletter argument, a recommendation weighing Lapland tourism and nature, and a concise team follow-up email. Learners arrange sentence blocks, hear the complete model in Finnish, review the English or Bangla meaning, and write a personal draft that autosaves in the browser. The draft is not scored. The examples follow OPH’s B2.1 written-production goal of expressing ideas fairly clearly and precisely across topics with varied structures; this is guided practice, not an official YKI writing task.

The B1 Grammar Lab adds four short pattern lessons: relative clauses with joka/jossa, experiences with the perfect tense, contrast with vaikka, and purpose clauses with jotta. Learners hear Finnish browser speech and choose a form in context, with English or Bangla feedback. This focused set complements OPH’s B1.1 description of connected expression and a broader range of structures; it is not a complete grammar syllabus.

The B2 Grammar Lab adds four contextual pattern lessons: the conditional for tentative proposals, the present passive for shared processes, zero-person sentences for general experience, and the plural partitive in phrases such as useita reittejä (“several routes”). Aalto University’s Finnish 3B course objectives include these grammar areas; the lessons here use them in more connected Finland-themed examples. OPH describes B2.1 broadly as clear, precise expression with varied structures. These four lessons are focused practice, not a complete B2 grammar syllabus.

The C1 Grammar Lab adds four short, Finland-themed pattern lessons: the active present participle as a compact relative clause, the agent participle, a past temporal substitute construction, and a reference construction that condenses a positive että-clause. Each lesson includes a Finnish example, pattern breakdown, browser audio, a multiple-choice check, and English or Bangla feedback. OPH describes C1.1 as flexible, precise, structured expression across many topics; its scale does not prescribe these specific grammar topics. The topics are a curated practice set, not a complete C1 syllabus. References: [OPH proficiency scale](https://www.oph.fi/fi/koulutus-ja-tutkinnot/kehittyvan-kielitaidon-tasojen-kuvausasteikko), [active present participle](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-grammar/verbs/participles/the-active-present-participle-va-partisiippi), [agent participle](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-grammar/verbs/participles/agent-participle-agenttipartisiippi), [temporal substitute construction](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-grammar/syntax/constructions/temporal-substitute-construction-temporaalinen-lauseenvastike), and [reference construction](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-grammar/verbs/participles/partisiipit-the-reference-construction-etta-participle).

The C2 Grammar Lab adds four advanced, Finland-themed practice lessons: case choice inside a relative clause, the case selected by suhtautua, the paired comparative pattern mitä… sitä…, and choosing standard or casual Finnish for the situation. Examples include English or Bangla guidance, a Finnish browser-audio model, and an answer explanation. The Council of Europe describes C2 as precise reformulation, nuanced expression, and consistent control of complex grammar, but does not prescribe these Finnish topics; this is a curated practice set, not a full C2 syllabus. References: [Council of Europe C2 descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-3-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-qualitative-aspects-of-spoken-language-use), [Aalto Finnish Online Grammar](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/course/view.php?id=158), and [Kotus on standard, spoken, and other language forms](https://kotus.fi/kielenhuolto/yleiskieli-ja-sen-kehitys/yleiskieli-ja-muut-kielimuodot/).

The advanced vocabulary shelf adds ten C1 and ten C2 Finnish words selected for the app’s themes of cities, nature, community decisions, and public services. Each word has an English and Bangla meaning, a contextual Finnish sentence and translations, browser audio, and an optional spaced-review card. The CEFR describes C1 as drawing on a broad range of language and C2 as precise expression of fine shades of meaning; these word sets are selected practice, not an official vocabulary list. References: [Council of Europe CEFR qualitative descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-3-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-qualitative-aspects-of-spoken-language-use) and [Kielitoimiston sanakirja](https://www.kielitoimistonsanakirja.fi/).

Kielo can be installed as a progressive web app. On supported browsers, an install prompt or browser-menu option adds it to a device's home screen. After one online visit, a service worker caches the static app shell, course data, and local cover image so the lessons remain available offline; progress continues to use browser-local storage. Unsplash photography, linked Yle media, and Finnish speech voices depend on network and browser support. The manifest and cache behavior follow [MDN's PWA installability guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Guides/Making_PWAs_installable) and [offline operation guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Guides/Offline_and_background_operation).

The C2 Reading Lab adds three longer original texts about a Helsinki library, a Lapland nature trail, and winter cycling. Questions ask learners to interpret what a writer implies, compare institutional viewpoints, and assess how a caveat changes the force of a public statement. Each passage includes selected word help, Finnish browser audio, full English or Bangla translation, and feedback. The focus follows OPH/YKI’s C2 description of understanding a wide range of written language and noticing subtle differences of tone and nuance; the texts are original practice, not official YKI tasks. Sources: [OPH/YKI proficiency scale](https://www.oph.fi/sites/default/files/documents/169247_proficiency_levels_cefr.pdf) and [Yle Uutiset](https://yle.fi/uutiset) for optional authentic reading.

Learners identify a main claim, weigh reasons and trade-offs, or infer a speaker’s position. Finnish browser speech is played at a near-normal rate; a Yle Areena podcast link provides an optional path to authentic full-speed recordings. The focused listening tasks align with OPH’s B2.1 descriptor for following longer speech and complex argumentation and identifying its main points, but they are not an official YKI test or complete B2 course.

Learners tap selected words for English or Bangla help, listen with browser speech, compare viewpoints, and answer questions about an argument or inference. The activities follow OPH’s B2.1 reading descriptor for understanding varied written texts, including abstract topics, facts, attitudes, and opinions; they are focused practice, not a full B2 course or official YKI task.

Learners use selected Finnish vocabulary with English or Bangla meanings, hear browser speech, and answer questions that require synthesis, inference, and attention to a writer’s stance. The practice follows OPH’s C1.1 reading descriptors for understanding complex texts, summarising main points, recognising attitudes, and evaluating what was read critically. Yle Uutiset offers optional authentic follow-up. This is a focused reading lab, not a complete C1 course or official YKI task.

The C1 Listening Lab adds three original, extended talks about renewable energy siting, cultural tourism in Lapland, and the role of public libraries. Learners infer a speaker’s position, follow qualifications, and distinguish broad claims from local effects. Browser speech synthesis provides repeatable practice; Yle Areena links to unscripted recorded Finnish. The activity follows OPH’s C1 listening descriptor for longer speech on unfamiliar topics, including less explicit structure, idiomatic language, and register shifts. It is a focused lab, not an official YKI task or a full C1 course.

The C2 Listening Lab adds three longer original talks about a neighbourhood sauna, winter cycling routes, and digital public services. Learners listen for what a speaker qualifies, what remains implied, and how a balanced position differs from a simple yes or no. The passages are played with browser-generated Finnish speech at a natural practice speed; transcripts and English or Bangla support appear after the answer. Yle Areena provides optional unscripted listening. The B2 Speaking Lab adds four guided Finland-set discussions: make a balanced case for winter cycling, weigh jobs and nature in Lapland, disagree politely about office days, and summarise a plan before confirming it. The C2 Speaking Lab adds four open-ended discussions about silence and social expectations, sauna as a cultural metaphor, access to digital library services, and winter cycling routes. Learners practise connotation, a carefully qualified idiom, and fluent rephrasing. English and Bangla support accompany Finnish partner prompts and model responses; optional browser speech recognition shows a transcript without scoring pronunciation or response quality. The goals follow the Council of Europe’s C2 oral-interaction descriptors for idiomatic language, shades of meaning, and smooth reformulation. These are guided examples, not official YKI tasks.

The C2 Writing Lab adds three original guided essays on winter mobility in Helsinki, tourism and local perspectives in northern Finland, and access to digital public services. Paragraph-order activities include English and Bangla model translations, hints, browser audio, and unscored personal drafts saved on-device. The tasks reflect the Council of Europe C2 reports-and-essays descriptors; these are practice models, not official YKI tasks.

The C1 Speaking Lab adds four open-ended, Finland-set discussions about Helsinki’s green corridors, Lapland tourism, hybrid work, and public libraries. Each scenario presents a partner’s view, then invites the learner to respond, qualify a claim, offer a condition, or broaden the decision criteria. Finnish model turns come with English and Bangla support; speech recognition shows a transcript without scoring it. The activities follow OPH’s C1.1 goals for fluent, precise interaction, connecting contributions, taking responsibility for discussion, and rephrasing when needed. They are guided practice, not a full C1 course or official YKI task.

The C1 Writing Lab completes the four-skills practice path through C1. Three structured text tasks address Helsinki housing and urban nature, Lapland tourism, and the role of public libraries. Learners sequence paragraphs, inspect a detailed Finnish model, listen with browser speech, and save a personal response locally. Drafts are not scored. The writing goals follow OPH’s C1 descriptors for well-structured, detailed writing on complex topics, highlighting key points and supporting opinions with reasons and examples. These are guided examples, not a complete C1 course or official YKI tasks.

## First release plan

1. **Research and planning:** this document records the teaching model, level map, content sources, and product assumptions.
2. **Mockup:** a responsive learner dashboard shows the current path, next lesson, practice streak, and a Finland-themed scene.
3. **Course experience:** level navigation covers Pre-A1 through C2; each level has a coherent progression, while playable lessons demonstrate the lesson, quiz, listening, speaking, and flashcard patterns.
4. **Culture and media:** season cards connect vocabulary to Finland; a media shelf links to Yle's beginner videos, levelled materials, and simplified news.
5. **Review and delivery:** check the local preview, publish the public GitHub Pages site, and push the source to the public GitHub repository.
6. **Practice iteration:** ship scene-specific pictures, picture matching, spaced word review, and guided café, train-station, and grocery-store conversations.

## Design and teaching choices

- Keep the core navigation and controls in English; offer Bangla/English lesson hints as a learner setting.
- Keep Finnish visible beside its translation. Use short sentences, one concept at a time, audio playback, image prompts, and immediate answer feedback.
- Make the daily word an easy retrieval-practice moment: offer three beginner-level meaning choices in the selected English or Bangla help language, then reveal both translations and the contextual sentence after the learner answers. Keep this daily self-check temporary rather than adding a score to saved progress.
- Use a progression that includes listening, speaking, reading, and writing. Speaking prompts are practice prompts, not an automated pronunciation score.
- Use learner-created examples and link to Yle for third-party video. External video availability and captions can change, so media cards identify their source and open the official material.
- Save lesson completion, conversation completion, and vocabulary review to this browser with `localStorage`; no account or server is needed for the first release.
- Show a gentle, seven-day study rhythm on the home dashboard. Record local calendar dates from learning actions (not language or course-level preferences) and include those dates in downloadable progress backups.

## Grocery-store conversation

The A1 grocery-store role-play uses short, standard Finnish responses for finding rye bread, understanding a card-payment prompt, and answering whether the learner wants a receipt. The practice is an original beginner adaptation, rather than a transcript. Yle’s authentic grocery-shopping material covers these everyday shop interactions at A2.1, so the app offers it as an optional next step: [Yle Oppiminen: Ruokakaupassa](https://yle.fi/a/20-152698).


## Library conversation

The A1 library role-play connects everyday Finnish with public-library culture in the Helsinki region. Its four short exchanges practise asking for a library card, describing a Finnish address, checking when to return a book, and understanding *eräpäivä* (due date). The example follows Helmet’s stated card and loan guidance, while the linked official page remains the source for current local rules: [Helmet: Library card and loans](https://helmet.finna.fi/Content/kirjastokortti-ja-lainaaminen?lng=en-gb).


## Open-response conversation practice

Every role-play now offers a choice between selecting a prepared reply and composing a short Finnish response. Learners reveal the model only after attempting their own answer. The app does not claim to grade free-form Finnish; the sample is a comparison point, and learner text stays in memory only and is not saved. This encourages retrieval and short-form production without marking valid alternate wording as wrong.


## Conversation word hints

Each open-response turn offers optional vocabulary cues with English or Bangla meanings. These give beginners a small prompt without replacing the example phrase or evaluating their response. Hint visibility and typed responses remain temporary in the current page.


## Full-scene conversation listening

Each beginner role-play now has a play-all control. The Finnish prompts and model replies are spoken one by one with short pauses, so learners can hear the rhythm of a complete café, train-station, grocery-store, or library exchange. The learner can stop playback or interrupt it with any other phrase audio. Playback uses the Finnish voice available in the browser and does not save progress.


## Bilingual conversation transcripts

Learners can open a full transcript for any of the four role-play scenes. Staff prompts and sample learner replies appear as alternating, individually playable Finnish lines, with translations that follow the English/Bangla help setting. The transcript is optional and stays within the current scene.


## Adjustable Finnish conversation speed

A conversation pace selector offers slow, steady, and natural rates. It applies to full-scene playback and individual Finnish lines in the conversation practice and transcript, so a learner can rehear a phrase at a more comfortable speed. When a scene is already playing, a newly selected rate applies from the next spoken line.


## Transcript meaning recall

The full-scene transcript now lets learners hide the English or Bangla meanings while they try to recall them from the Finnish. They can reveal the meanings again without leaving the conversation or interrupting the learning path.


## Finnish-first conversation practice

An optional audio-first setting hides the Finnish prompt translation and reply translations until the learner selects a response. After an answer, the Finnish line and current English/Bangla meanings appear for reflection. The learner can switch back to captioned practice at any time.


## Spoken open-response practice

The role-play writing mode now offers optional Finnish speech recognition where the browser supports it. The browser transcript is placed in the editable response box so learners can compare their own words with the model; it is not evaluated or saved. A short notice explains that browser speech recognition may process audio through its own service. Text input remains available when voice recognition is unsupported or permission is unavailable.


## Everyday spoken Finnish

The A1 Spoken Finnish lab compares five informal dialogue examples with standard written forms, with browser audio and English or Bangla explanations. It uses beginner examples from [Aalto OpenLearning’s question practice](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/mod/page/view.php?id=4673), which covers forms such as *mä oon*, *ootsä?*, *haluut sä kahvii?*, and *kahvii / maitoo*. The module presents these as forms to recognise, not a single nationwide dialect; standard Finnish remains a useful written form. Aalto OpenLearning material is marked CC BY 4.0 unless otherwise stated, and the site links to its own example videos.

## Finnish character quick keys

Lesson drafts, writing-lab drafts, and open role-play replies now include accessible buttons for ä, ö, å, Ä, Ö, and Å. Selecting a key inserts that letter at the text cursor or replaces the current selection; the existing draft input flow remains active.

## Bilingual Finnish draft self-review

Lesson drafts, writing-lab drafts, and open role-play replies now include an optional three-item checklist in English or Bangla. It asks learners to explain their meaning, recheck ä/ö and long or doubled sounds, and compare word endings with the lesson example. It is reflective guidance only: it neither scores nor edits a learner’s writing. The pronunciation prompt is informed by [Aalto OpenLearning’s beginner Finnish pronunciation guide](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/mod/page/view.php?id=3388).

## Progress-aware next step

The home dashboard now sends learners directly to a spaced-review session when saved words are due, then resumes the lesson path when review is caught up. Completing all 28 lessons changes the home action to level exploration. Study-rhythm language treats breaks as normal and clarifies that progress is saved on the current device.

## Finnish word of the day

The home page selects one existing Pre-A1 or A1 course word for each local calendar day. The stable daily rotation uses beginner words that appear in their lesson phrase, pairs each word and sentence with English and Bangla meanings and the lesson’s Finland scene, and offers normal-speed word audio plus normal and slower playback for the example sentence. The meanings start hidden for an optional active-recall pause and are revealed on demand in English and Bangla. A learner can speak the word before listening and mark the self-check complete; the practice is self-reported, uses no microphone, and is not stored. They can also add or remove the word from the existing local spaced-review shelf; the recall state is temporary and no additional learner data is stored.

## Kielo A–Z redesign plan (2026-10-22)

### Product promise

Make Finnish useful by teaching it through everyday life in Finland. Keep the interface in English, offer English and Bangla learning support, and connect each language unit to a real place, institution, season, or activity. The learning path runs from Pre-A1 to C2 and develops listening, speaking, reading, writing, grammar, vocabulary, and cultural understanding together.

### Information architecture

1. **Home** — Finnish local time and date first; current weather with a selector covering every Finnish region beside it; today's 100-word study plan; continue course; review due words; progress; one Finland fact or scene.
2. **Learn Finnish** — Pre-A1–C2 sequence; four skills; pronunciation and grammar; themed lessons; dialogues; daily review; saved vocabulary.
3. **Daily 100** — a dated, deterministic set of 100 distinct words; ten short groups of ten; beginner-to-advanced level labels; Finnish audio, English meaning, Bangla support where curated, examples when available, save/review, session progress, and a ten-question recall quiz after each word group with a missed-word retry. The set changes on the Finnish calendar date. Do not present a word-list lemma as a translation or a definition.
4. **Dictionary** — Finnish-to-English lookup and English-to-Finnish lookup over licensed open lexical data; search Finnish inflected forms when the source corpus supports them; show source, part of speech, and inflection notes when available; mark unreviewed machine-derived or community definitions clearly; retain curated English/Bangla learner entries as a higher-confidence layer. Link to Kotus for authoritative usage guidance. The app will say "open learner dictionary" and disclose corpus coverage; no finite list is described as every Finnish word.
5. **Explore Finland** — connected language-and-place chapters: Finnish history; regions and geography; people, languages and society; government and public services; seasons, climate and weather; forests and wildlife; lakes, Baltic Sea and archipelago; transport and road safety; universities and study; jobs and work life; housing and home life; health services and wellbeing; food, markets and daily shopping; cities, landmarks and travel; sauna, holidays and media. Each chapter combines a short factual overview, vocabulary, useful phrases, an image or licensed/official media link, and a source/last-checked date.
6. **Practice** — spaced repetition; picture and listening activities; phrase building; conversation role-play; writing and reading labs; progress and export.

### Research decisions and data rules

- Course progression continues to use OPH/YKI and Council of Europe CEFR guidance; lessons remain original practice, not official examination material.
- Use the Nykysuomen sanalista as a licensed Finnish headword and inflection resource, not as a source of English/Bangla definitions. It has over 100,000 entries, is not exhaustive or normative, is updated periodically, and is CC BY 4.0.
- For a broad Finnish–English lookup, evaluate an openly licensed compiled corpus based on FinnWordNet and Wiktionary. Preserve its included attribution, CC BY-SA terms, source/version date, and modification notice. Search coverage and definitions vary; Kotus remains the usage authority. Add Bangla translations and learner examples as original editorial content, not as if Kotus supplied them.
- Finnish civil and population facts are dated and link to Statistics Finland and official Finland sources. Weather is transient and can fail independently of learning content.
- Use `Europe/Helsinki` for time and the same zone for date-based daily sets. Weather starts at Helsinki and offers one representative city for each of Finland's 19 official regions, labelled with both city and region; coordinates are resolved to Open-Meteo's Finnish settlement results. No browser geolocation prompt. Show attribution and a human-readable last-updated state; refresh on an interval, with a manual refresh and cached last result. If the request fails, keep the clock/course working and show the last weather update or an unavailable state. The selector covers regions, not every municipality.
- Open-Meteo's free API is restricted to non-commercial use and requires CC BY 4.0 attribution. The public Pages/Sites build is treated as a non-commercial educational project for this iteration. If the site is monetized, change to an appropriately licensed commercial endpoint before that use.
- Device voices remain a no-account fallback. The Web Speech API exposes a voice name and locale but no dependable gender field. The app now has an optional server-side Azure Speech path for documented Finnish neural voices; its female/male labels come from Microsoft’s voice table, not browser heuristics. A short bilingual sample lets learners audition the selected installed or neural voice before using it in lessons. The neural path stays off unless the Site owner provides a Speech resource key, matching region, and daily character cap.
- Use official/rights-cleared images and video links. Link to Yle and official institutions instead of copying or rehosting their media.
- Account-free progress stays in local storage. Weather city, course progress, saved words, and activity history remain on that device. Any future account/cloud sync requires a separate privacy design.

### Neural Finnish voice research and implementation (checked 2026-10-22)

Microsoft’s current voice support table lists Finnish (Finland) standard neural voices: female `fi-FI-SelmaNeural` and `fi-FI-NooraNeural`, and male `fi-FI-HarriNeural`. The speech REST endpoint requires a Speech resource key and a matching region. North Europe (`northeurope`) supports neural text-to-speech. Azure’s published Free F0 table currently includes 0.5 million neural text-to-speech characters per month; Azure also publishes paid per-million-character prices, which vary by offer. The app’s hard daily cap is 10,000 characters (310,000 in a 31-day month), and Site owners may set a lower cap. Other usage on the same Speech resource can use the shared quota, so owners should review their current plan before enabling voices.

The worker at `worker/speech.ts` accepts only the three allowlisted voice IDs, text up to 180 characters, and `slow`/`steady`/`natural` pacing. Only the Sites origin, GitHub Pages origin, and local preview origins are allowed through browser CORS. The key remains a Sites runtime secret. The endpoint is disabled unless the key, matching region, D1 binding, and a positive cap are present. A D1 row tracks only total characters and request count for the Europe/Helsinki day; it stores no phrase text, IP address, or account identifier. Voice mode defaults to device speech, and the client calls Azure only after an explicit neural-voice choice and a listen action. Missing configuration, provider failure, or a reached cap falls back to the device voice when installed.
